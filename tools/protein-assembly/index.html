Pack proteins into micelles

Load a protein with a globular head and a hydrophobic tail, choose how many copies you want, and watch the tails bury themselves together while the heads crowd at the surface.

Check every result. This tool was vibe coded: built quickly with AI assistance and not independently validated. It is a coarse geometric model, not a physics-grade simulation. Compare its output with your own data and standard software before using it in figures, analyses or publications.

1Load a protein

Open a PDB or mmCIF file, or fetch one by PDB ID (for example 1AKI), AlphaFold code (for example AF-P28635-F1) or UniProt accession.

Takes a model file from the AlphaFold Database (.pdb or .cif), or the whole .zip download from AlphaFold Server or ColabFold (it picks model 0 or rank 1; you can switch). Confidence (pLDDT) is read from the B-factor column and shown under the sequence.

+Add molecules (optional)

Other molecules, such as a drug, a dye or a small protein, drawn as spheres sized from their diameter. They never overlap anything. Changes apply the next time you press Build and run or Calculate (step 4).

Enter a CAS number and press Look up to fill in the name, diameter and behavior from PubChem; you can still edit them. Rough diameters by hand: small drug or dye 0.8–1.5 nm; peptide 1.5–2 nm; for a globular protein, diameter ≈ 0.132 × (mass in Da)1/3 nm, so 10 kDa ≈ 2.8 nm and 50 kDa ≈ 4.9 nm. Hydrophobicity sets how strongly a molecule is drawn to the tails: 0 is inert (it only takes up space), 1 is as sticky as a tail residue, 2 is twice as sticky.

+Add phospholipid (optional)

Each phospholipid is a sphere for the head group, sized from the molecule like the molecules above, plus one bead chain per acyl chain. The chains act just like the protein tails: same bonds, and the same hydrophobic attraction to the tails and to each other, so lipids can join the micelle. Changes apply the next time you press Build and run or Calculate (step 4).

Enter a CAS number and press Look up: PubChem gives the name, the molecular weight and the structure, from which the acyl chains (carbons per chain) are read. For example POPC 26853-31-6, DPPC 63-89-8, DOPC 4235-95-4. Head group size = whole-molecule volume (molecular weight × 1.66 Å³ per g/mol, a lipid density of 1 g/cm³) minus the acyl chains (27 Å³ per CH₂, 54 Å³ per CH₃), drawn as a sphere of that volume. Each chain bead stands for about 3.5 carbons. Chain stickiness 1 means as sticky as a tail residue. Set a chain to 0 for a lysolipid.

2Choose the hydrophobic tail

The tail must run to one end of the chain. Everything else is treated as the rigid globular head.

Auto-detect looks for a terminal stretch that sticks out from the folded head (few long-range contacts) or, in AlphaFold models, has low confidence (pLDDT below 50). You can also click a residue below: the tail then runs from that residue to the nearest chain end.

Useful when the tail is missing from a crystal structure. The typed tail replaces nothing; the whole chain becomes the head.

How the model works

Beads, forces and moves

The head keeps its real shape from the structure and moves as a rigid body (it can slide and rotate). The tail is rebuilt as a flexible chain of beads 3.8 Å apart, starting at the anchor residue, so its pose in the file does not matter.

Tail beads attract one another with a short-range well (an implicit-solvent model in the style of Cooke, Kremer and Deserno, 2005), which stands in for the hydrophobic effect. With the "By sequence" setting the strength between two residues scales with the Kyte–Doolittle hydrophobicity of both, so charged or polar residues in the tail stick less. Heads only repel, so they stay hydrated on the outside. Water is not modeled explicitly.

No overlaps. Each head is represented by beads that enclose all of its atoms and its drawn surface, and each tail residue is a 2.8 Å sphere. After the forces move everything, an overlap check pushes apart anything still overlapping (tails move directly, heads move as rigid bodies) until the deepest overlap is under 0.02 Å; the 3D view, the tables and the PDB export are all taken after this check. The only contact allowed is between a tail's first residue and its own head, where the chain leaves the surface, and between neighboring residues along the tail, which form one continuous tube. The "Deepest overlap" readout shows the check is working.

Moves follow overdamped Langevin dynamics at a temperature of 1 kT. The hydrophobic strength slider sets the depth of the tail–tail well in kT; stronger values make assemblies form faster but anneal less.

Added molecules

Each added molecule is a rigid sphere with the diameter you give. Its hydrophobicity h scales the same short-range attraction the tail residues feel: the well depth with a tail residue is the hydrophobic strength × √h (× √ of that residue's own stickiness), and between two molecules it is the strength × √(h₁h₂). h = 0 means it only repels; h = 1 matches a tail residue. When a micelle is built directly, molecules with h ≥ 0.5 start in the core. A molecule counts as part of an assembly when it is within 2 Å of one of its beads, and as "core" when that contact is a tail. With a CAS number, the name, mass, formula and XLogP come from PubChem, and h is set to XLogP ÷ 4 (0 to 2); the diameter is that of a sphere with the PubChem 3D conformer volume (or 1.25 Å³ per dalton when there is no 3D conformer). The PDB export writes each molecule as one HETATM pseudo-atom (chain Z) with its radius in the B-factor column.

Packing parameter and copy limits

v is the summed residue volume of the tail (Zamyatnin volumes), l is the tail length from the setting above, and a₀ is the cross-section of a sphere with the head's volume. Classic ranges: p < ⅓ spherical micelles, ⅓–½ rods or worm-like micelles, ½–1 bilayers or vesicles.

The core limit is how many tails fit in a sphere of radius l. The shell limit is how many heads fit around that core when packed hexagonally. The smaller of the two is the predicted maximum copies per micelle; with more copies you should expect several assemblies or non-spherical ones.

Phospholipids

A phospholipid is one head-group sphere plus a bead chain for each acyl chain (about 3.5 carbons per bead, 3.8 Å bonds, a soft bend stiffness so chains stay fairly straight). The head group is sized from the molecule: the whole-molecule volume (molecular weight × 1.66 Å³ per g/mol) minus the acyl-chain volume (27 Å³ per CH₂, 54 Å³ per CH₃). Head groups are hydrophilic (they only take up space). Chain beads use exactly the tail rules: they repel protein heads, and attract tail beads and other chain beads with the hydrophobic strength × the chain stickiness. When a micelle is built directly, lipids start with their head groups at the core surface and chains pointing in; in self-assembly they start anywhere. A lipid counts as part of an assembly when its head group touches it or one of its chain beads touches a tail.

Crosslinks

Crosslink joins neighbouring copies with the number of linkers you enter (default 10). Each linker is two residues (two red beads, three 3.8 Å bonds) between surface residues of two different copies that are 0.5–1.8 nm apart, preferring residues on the outside of the assembly. The bonds pull on both rigid heads, so linked copies move together and their relative motion is restricted; linker beads cannot pass through the two proteins they join. Linked copies count as one assembly and no longer make long-range hops. Press Crosslink again to add more; Remove crosslinks takes them all away, and Blow up or Restart also removes them.

What it does not capture

No specific head–head interfaces, charges, salt, pH or concentration effects, no secondary structure in the tail and no explicit water. Treat the shapes as geometrically plausible arrangements, not predictions of a real complex.

Files are processed in your browser and are never uploaded. Structures fetched by ID come from the RCSB PDB or the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database. The 3D view uses the open-source three.js library, loaded from jsDelivr.
